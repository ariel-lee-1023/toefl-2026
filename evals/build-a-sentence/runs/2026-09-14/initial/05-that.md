# 05-that

Classify `that` by its role in the sentence, not just by nearby reporting verbs.

| Example | Role of `that` | Structural evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The note **that she copied** vanished. | Relative pronoun introducing a relative clause | The clause modifies `note`: she copied **the note**. Inside the relative clause, `that` supplies the object role linked to that noun. |
| The claim **that she resigned** surprised us. | Marker introducing a content clause | `She resigned` is a complete proposition. It tells us the content of the claim; there is no missing object referring to `claim`. |
| **That note** vanished. | Demonstrative determiner | `That` identifies which note; it is followed by a noun, not a subordinate clause. |

The second example has a content clause after a noun, `claim`, without a reporting verb. A reporting verb is therefore unnecessary for this classification. It is also insufficient: “He said that note vanished” contains reporting verb `said`, but `that` still determines `note`.

Use two checks: does the clause supply a proposition, or does it modify a noun and contain a role linked to that noun? If there is no clause after `that`, check whether it points to a referent as a demonstrative. These are introductory categories, not an exhaustive account of every use.
