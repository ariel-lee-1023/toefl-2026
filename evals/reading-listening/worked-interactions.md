# Worked interactions

These are authored demonstrations, not recorded learner performance or independent evaluation results. All learner replies below are illustrative. Actual forward-test outputs are recorded separately in the evaluation report.

## Familiar reading and precise paraphrases

**Learner request:** I have done every question. Help me study this passage again.

**Supplied practice sentence:** Members may enter the laboratory early only if a supervisor is present.

**Coach, first turn:** Let's reuse it for a new meaning task. Which statement preserves the condition: A, members are required to enter early when supervised; or B, early entry is permitted only with supervision? Reply with A or B and, if useful, the words that guided you.

**Illustrative learner:** A, because the supervisor is present.

**Coach, hint turn:** Does “may enter” express a requirement or permission? Try your choice again with that distinction in mind.

**Illustrative learner:** B. “May” means permission.

**Coach, feedback and variation:** Yes. B preserves permission and the necessary supervision condition. A changes permission into obligation. Now change only “may” to “must”: “Members must enter the laboratory early.” Is early entry optional or required?

The coach stops here for the next attempt. It neither demands a past score nor treats this familiar sentence as an independent proficiency measurement.

## Listening notes and retrieval

**Learner request:** Guide my listening notes using the announcement.

**Coach, first turn:** This is an original campus announcement. Listen once and keep any notes you find useful. Tell me when you have finished; I will then show the question. Play `examples/announcement-audio/audio.wav` through the host's audio player. The script and key stay private during this turn. This file passed rendering and non-silence checks; acoustic word-by-word review is pending.

**Illustrative learner:** Finished. Notes: rain / library / two / card / Friday / email.

**Coach, question turn:** If you cannot attend tomorrow, what should you do? A, cancel online tomorrow; B, email the coordinator today; C, attend the outdoor session tomorrow; D, return your student card. Reply with the option and the note you consulted.

**Illustrative learner:** B. I used “email,” but had to scan my notes twice to find it.

**Coach, feedback:** Correct. The script directs anyone unable to attend tomorrow to email the coordinator today. Your note preserves the action but not its condition or deadline. The repeated scan may reflect cue layout, though this one attempt does not establish a stable problem. Keep your original notes, then compare this retrospective model made with the script:

```text
CHANGE: tomorrow indoors, library / WHY: rain
TIME: still 2
BRING: student card -> registration check
OUTDOOR: next Fri
IF miss tomorrow -> email coordinator TODAY, not cancel online
```

**Coach, next step:** On a fresh comparable recording, try placing conditional actions on their own line. Use the actual practice timer and note whether you find the cue and submit in time. This retrospective model is not a claim about what you could write during one playback.

The original notes remain distinct from the model. This is listening-note coaching, not an Active Cognitive Buffer episode.
