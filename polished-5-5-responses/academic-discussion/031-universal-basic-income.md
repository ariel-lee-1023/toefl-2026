# Universal Basic Income

## Prompt (including both student posts)
Your professor is teaching a class on economics. Write a post responding to the professor’s question.

In your response, you should do the following:
- Express and support your opinion.
- Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

An effective response will contain at least 100 words.

Dr. Gupta

We’ve been discussing the concept of universal basic income (UBI), where all citizens receive a regular, unconditional sum of money from the government. Some economists believe UBI can reduce poverty and stimulate economic growth, while others argue it may discourage people from working and contributing to society. What are your thoughts on implementing universal basic income? Would it be beneficial or harmful? Please explain.

Claire

I think UBI would be beneficial. It would provide financial security for everyone, especially the most vulnerable. It could help reduce poverty and give people the freedom to pursue education and entrepreneurial activities without the constant stress of financial instability.

Paul

In my opinion, UBI might be harmful. It could reduce the incentive for people to work and contribute to the economy. Additionally, funding such a program could require significant tax increases, which might negatively impact economic growth.

## My Polished Response
While Claire rightly emphasizes financial security, I agree with Paul that UBI could create serious economic risks. First, providing payments to everyone, including wealthy citizens, could consume funds that might otherwise support those in greatest need. If financed through substantially higher business taxes, the program could also leave firms with less money to invest. For example, a small manufacturer might postpone buying equipment or hiring workers, limiting its capacity to expand. Second, if payments were sufficient to cover basic expenses, some people might choose to work fewer hours because they would depend less on wages. If this occurred widely, reduced production could weaken the tax base needed to sustain the program. These outcomes are not inevitable, but they make me favor targeted assistance over universal payments. This would prioritize Claire’s concern for vulnerable people while limiting the financial commitment Paul questions.
