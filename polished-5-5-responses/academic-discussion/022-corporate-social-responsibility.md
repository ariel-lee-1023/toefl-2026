# Corporate Social Responsibility

## Prompt (including both student posts)
Your professor is teaching a class on business ethics. Write a post responding to the professor's question.

**In your response, you should do the following.**

- Express and support your opinion.
- Make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

An effective response will contain at least 100 words.

Dr. Okafor

We've been discussing the concept of corporate social responsibility (CSR), the idea that businesses have an obligation to act in ways that benefit society beyond simply generating profits. Some business leaders believe that companies should actively invest in social and environmental causes as part of their core mission. Others argue that the primary responsibility of a business is to maximize profits for its shareholders. Which perspective do you agree with?

Jordan

I think businesses should absolutely take social responsibility seriously. Companies have enormous influence and resources, and they should use that power to make a positive impact on the communities they serve. Investing in environmental sustainability, fair labor practices, and community programs builds trust with consumers and creates long-term value for everyone.

Lena

I believe a company's main job is to be profitable and provide value to its shareholders. When businesses try to take on too many social causes, it can distract from their core operations and reduce efficiency. Companies serve society best by creating jobs, offering quality products, and driving economic growth.

## My Polished Response
While Jordan rightly notes that social responsibility can build public trust, I agree with Lena that a company's primary objective should be long-term profitability. Financial stability enables businesses to sustain employment, improve products, and withstand economic downturns. Jordan's argument, however, gives insufficient attention to companies with limited resources. A small manufacturer, for example, may need to choose between sponsoring a community event and replacing unreliable machinery. Prioritizing the machinery would prevent production delays and protect jobs, benefiting the community through a stronger local employer. This does not make ethical conduct optional. Fair treatment of workers and honest advertising should remain basic obligations, regardless of their immediate financial return. Such practices can also support profitability by reducing staff turnover and encouraging repeat purchases. The key distinction is between conducting business responsibly and funding additional social initiatives. Companies should uphold the former as part of their everyday operations, while undertaking the latter only when doing so would not compromise their financial stability.
