Healthcare and Social Mobility

## Prompt

Dr. Diaz

We’ve been discussing the concept of social mobility. Some experts argue that attaining a higher education is the key to improving social mobility, while others believe that universal access to quality healthcare and social services plays a more crucial role. Which factor do you think is more important for enhancing social mobility? Why?

Andrew

I think higher education is more important for enhancing social mobility. A college degree can open doors to better job opportunities and higher earnings, which can significantly improve one’s social and economic status over time.

Kelly

In my opinion, access to quality healthcare and social services is more crucial. Without good health and basic support, individuals may struggle to pursue education and career opportunities. Ensuring that everyone has access to these services can create a more level playing field.

## My Polished Response
While Andrew is right that a college degree can improve earning potential, I agree with Kelly that universal access to quality healthcare and social services is more fundamental to social mobility. These services give low-income families the stability to pursue opportunities whose benefits take time to materialize. For example, a student may have to leave college to work full time if a parent's illness results in both lost income and unaffordable medical bills. Accessible treatment and temporary income support could help the family manage that crisis, allowing the student to remain enrolled and complete a qualification that improves future job prospects. Without such support, an opportunity to escape poverty could disappear because of circumstances unrelated to the student's ability or effort. Higher education remains valuable, but its benefits are limited when disadvantaged people cannot afford to complete it. Healthcare and social services therefore deserve priority because they make opportunities for advancement more accessible to those who face the greatest barriers.
