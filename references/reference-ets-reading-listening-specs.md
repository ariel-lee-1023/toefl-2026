## Reading task families

- **Complete the Words:** restore missing word endings using letters, grammar, vocabulary, and text meaning.
- **Read in Daily Life:** practical messages, notices, and informational texts; understand purpose, details, and implications.
- **Read an Academic Passage:** follow an academic explanation, its central point, supporting information, and vocabulary in context. All needed information is in the text; subject expertise is unnecessary.

The ETS Overview describes a 70–100-word academic paragraph with an intact first sentence, then second-half deletion in every second word, for ten incomplete words. Read in Daily Life samples use short practical texts and two- or three-item sets; Academic Passage samples use about 200 words and five questions. Listening samples use four response choices; a Choose a Response prompt is heard rather than printed. These are format references for original practice, not permission to reconstruct missing official items. Verify mask lengths against the intended words rather than copying underscores from PDF extraction.

## Listening task families

- **Listen and Choose a Response:** select a suitable reply to the utterance's meaning and communicative intent.
- **Listen to a Conversation:** follow two speakers, their purposes, details, and implications.
- **Listen to an Announcement:** identify the message's purpose, practical details, implications, and likely next actions.
- **Listen to an Academic Talk:** understand main/supporting ideas, organization, inferences, and contextual vocabulary.

ETS permits listening notes and includes English accents from several regions. No special subject background is assumed. The Overview's announcement example describes approximately 40–85 words. Do not turn the course's “no notes for short audio” preference into an official ban.

## Presentation and scoring boundaries

The blueprint specifies two-stage adaptive Reading and Listening. Reading texts remain visible. Listening stimuli play once, with questions following the stimulus. Replay and transcript study are useful assistance conditions, not an independent first attempt. The blueprint lists machine scoring and a maximum of one point per scored Reading/Listening item, with some unscored items included. It does not supply a conversion from this coach's raw accuracy to an official score.

The current content page lists Reading at 50 items/about 30 minutes and Listening at 47 items/about 29 minutes; these are base figures and may vary with adaptation. Official section and overall scores use 1–6. Do not apply the Writing/Speaking 0–5 constructed-response rubric to comprehension choices.

Use the timer and navigation rules of the actual practice interface. Do not promise a universal number of seconds per question, backward navigation, replay, or a fixed module count derived from course narration. A generated drill can use an agreed timer, labelled as practice timing. Neither word count nor a model's quality check calibrates difficulty or validates a test psychometrically.

## Coaching versus official mechanics

The other Reading/Listening references synthesize the supplied course methods or introduce original exercises. Rereading, optional note labels, guided questions, paraphrase repair, and spaced variations are teaching choices. For an exam-aligned item, follow verified mechanics; for a micro-exercise, explain its narrower skill goal. A personal learning record is not an ETS score report.
